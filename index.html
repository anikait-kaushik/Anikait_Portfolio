04 Selected work

Flagship Planetary robotics

Mars Rover “Ganga” — one robot, multiple mission-critical jobs.

International Rover Challenge 2026 · Champion · Team MARS, TIET

Ganga was not built as a rover that simply drives. It was engineered as an integrated planetary system — a platform that had to move through difficult terrain, interact with its environment, manipulate hardware, deploy instruments, collect and analyse samples, and execute mission tasks with increasing levels of autonomy.

My work on Ganga exposed me to the rover as a complete mechatronic system rather than a collection of independent subsystems. The platform brought together mobility, mechanical mechanisms, manipulation, sensing, instrument deployment, autonomous operations and mission-level integration — where a decision in one subsystem could directly affect what the rover could accomplish in the field.

A major part of that system was the Astrobiology Expedition (ABEx) task. I led the mechanical and astrobiology-expedition development around the soil-sampling and analysis workflow, taking it through mechanism design, CAD, fabrication, integration and field testing. The system had to excavate soil, transfer a representative sample and support the downstream analysis sequence as part of the rover's larger mission.

Beyond ABEx, the rover's engineering challenge was the integration of manipulation, instrument deployment and autonomous task execution into one field-ready platform. That meant designing for the handoff between mechanisms, sensors, electronics, software and operators — not just making an individual mechanism work on a workbench.

The result: Ganga became a field-deployed planetary robotics platform capable of tackling a broad set of mission tasks — and Team MARS finished 1st at IRC 2026.

02 · Planetary Rover

Alaknanda — Where I learned to engineer the mission.

IRC 2025 · 1st Runner-Up internationally

Before Ganga, Alaknanda taught me that a rover is only as good as the task it can complete. For the ABEx mission, I translated the requirement into a working chain: soil penetration → sample extraction → sensing → analysis.

I designed around the real engineering constraints — guided vertical motion, alignment, actuation, packaging and subsystem integration — so the mechanism could do more than move: it had to perform reliably in the field.

ClimbCart — The staircase is the terrain.

Engineering major project · 2026 – Present

Most load carriers assume the ground is flat. ClimbCart started with a different question: what if the terrain is a staircase — narrow, uneven and shaped by the constraints of an Indian home?

I took it from site and market observations through mechanism development, CAD, FEA, material selection, prototyping and testing around a TPU-chain track drive and self-levelling platform.
