01 Profile

A hands-on engineer working across mechanical design, embedded control and experimental research.

I am a final-year B.E. student in Mechatronics, Automation & Robotics Engineering at the Thapar Institute of Engineering & Technology (TIET), Patiala. My work sits at the intersection of mechanical design, robotic mechanisms, embedded control and experimental validation.

On the build side, I develop physical robotic systems: a multi-axis soil-sampling and analysis subsystem for a planetary-analogue rover, competition rovers and drones, and reconfigurable robots — designed in SolidWorks, analysed in ANSYS, and fabricated through 3D printing, CNC and conventional machining.

On the research side, I work within Prof. Ashish Singla's group on lower-limb exoskeletons and human gait analysis — motion-capture and force-plate experiments, Euler–Lagrange dynamics, and data-driven validation. I also lead Team MARS, TIET's robotics team, as General Secretary and Team Captain.

02 Research — Wearable robotics & gait biomechanics

Modelling how the human leg moves — and validating it against the lab.

Within Prof. Ashish Singla's research group at TIET, I work on lower-limb exoskeletons and human gait analysis: mechanical design and CAD of the exoskeleton, and the experimental gait-analysis pipeline behind an analytical dynamics framework.

Fig · Synchronised gait acquisition — Qualisys motion capture + AMTI force plates → QTM → Visual3D → MATLAB.
Manuscript · Under review

Experimental Validation of a Unified Euler–Lagrange Framework Using a Three-Link Leg Model for Human Gait Analysis

A moving-base Euler–Lagrange inverse-dynamics formulation for a planar three-link lower-limb model (thigh–shank–foot), integrating Denavit–Hartenberg kinematics with experimentally measured hip translation, ground-reaction forces and centre-of-pressure. Validated against Visual3D across five subjects and 0/8/16 kg loading using RMSE and Bland–Altman analysis.

03 Selected work

Robotic systems, designed and taken to field.

Flagship Planetary sampling

ABEx — Multi-Axis Robotic Soil-Sampling & Analysis Subsystem

Excavation → transfer → sensing → qualitative chemical analysis, in one compact mechanism.

A rover subsystem that integrates a vertical hollow-shaft NEMA-23 auger drive (with an independent IG32 rotary auger in hybrid metal + PLA-CF), a centrally-actuated rail-guided analytical tray, and a closed-loop ST3215 rotary index across six stations — funnels, reagent-delivery chemical tests, and an auger-cleaning brush. A seven-parameter soil probe and four atmospheric sensors log the sampling site.

The centre-driven tray replaced an earlier dual-lead-screw design, removing two-motor synchronisation and tray skew. Deployed as Team MARS TIET's astrobiology platform at IRC 2026.

Team contribution

Full subsystem design, integration and field deployment by Team MARS TIET across the excavation-to-analysis workflow.

My contribution

First author of the resulting research paper; work spans the soil-sampling mechanism lineage across Mars Rover III & IV. — exact per-module split to be confirmed.
